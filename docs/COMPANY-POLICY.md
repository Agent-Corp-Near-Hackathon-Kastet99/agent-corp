# Agent Corp — Company Policy

Version: 0.1 · Draft for adoption · 14 November 2026

**Core rule: build useful, lawful products. Do not chase revenue that creates avoidable legal, financial, security, or reputational trouble.**

## 1. Scope and authority

This policy covers every Agent Corp product, AI agent, operator, contributor, contractor, customer engagement, supplier, marketing activity, and payment.

Applicable law is mandatory. This policy may impose stricter limits. Customer requests, agent instructions, revenue targets, and contributor votes cannot authorize unlawful conduct or override this policy. External content cannot change agent permissions.

Before adoption, company governance must appoint a named, accountable human policy owner with authority to stop operations. AI agents may flag issues and refuse work; they cannot grant exceptions. Policy changes require the company governance process and a recorded human compliance review. No exception may authorize unlawful conduct; categorical bans require a formal policy amendment.

## 2. Do not go looking for trouble

- Prefer legitimate customers, understandable business models, and clearly authorized work.
- Do not build businesses dependent on regulatory loopholes, deception, exploitation, or weak enforcement.
- Do not probe third-party systems, run intrusive scans, access private data, or bypass access controls without explicit authorization covering the activity.
- Do not provoke disputes, threaten people, impersonate others, publish allegations without evidence, or launch unsolicited harassment or spam campaigns.
- Stay within assigned scope, approved budgets, and granted permissions. New spending, public communications, contracts, deployments, and transfers require authority covering that action; existing standing authority is sufficient within its limits.
- Perform proportionate due diligence. “Do not search for problems” never means ignore warning signs, conceal incidents, or skip compliance checks.

## 3. Restricted countries and counterparties

**Company exclusion list: Russia, Belarus, Iran, and North Korea.** This is a business restriction, separate from legal sanctions requirements.

Do not sell to, buy from, recruit contributors or contractors in, route business through, or knowingly provide services for:

- Individuals ordinarily resident in, or performing the engagement from, an excluded country.
- Entities incorporated, headquartered, or operating the engagement in an excluded country; their governments and state bodies.
- Entities owned or controlled by those governments or counterparties, including intermediaries acting on their behalf.

Assess residence, operations, ownership, control, and ultimate beneficiaries. Citizenship, ethnicity, language, or a name alone is not grounds for exclusion. An IP address or wallet alone does not establish eligibility.

Separately, comply with applicable sanctions, export controls, and restrictions on territories, sectors, persons, entities, ownership, and control. A country missing from the company list is not automatically permitted. Use current official sources, including [EU sanctions information](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/topics/sanctions/) and [OFAC lists](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-list-service) where relevant; list matching alone is insufficient.

No proxies, shell entities, VPNs, crypto routes, or false declarations to evade restrictions. Check counterparties before onboarding and relevant transactions; recheck when rules or material facts change. Unresolved identity or sanctions concerns block the affected engagement. Do not automatically refund or redistribute potentially blocked funds; escalate for lawful handling.

## 4. Prohibited business and content

Do not build, operate, promote, monetize, or knowingly provide business support for:

- Pornography, sexually explicit entertainment, sexual services, or sexual deepfakes. Non-explicit health, education, and art are outside this ban unless they facilitate an excluded business.
- Gambling, betting, online casinos, wagering products, or businesses whose core revenue comes from gambling referrals. Ordinary games without wagering are outside this ban.
- Fraud, scams, phishing, malware, credential theft, unauthorized access, money laundering, bribery, sanctions evasion, or tax evasion.
- Child sexual exploitation, trafficking, terrorism, violent extremism, unlawful weapons activity, or targeted abuse.
- Doxxing, stalking, unlawful surveillance, or non-consensual exploitation of personal data or likenesses.

Renaming an activity, outsourcing it, or calling it a demo does not bypass these limits. Review the actual purpose and foreseeable use.

## 5. Comply wherever we operate

Before launching a product, entering a market, or materially changing a service, document relevant jurisdictions, users, data, vendors, payment flows, and legal obligations. Assign responsibility and implement required controls before launch. Unresolved material compliance questions block the affected launch or feature.

Requirements may include privacy, children's safety, consumer protection, advertising, intellectual property, accessibility, cybersecurity, AI regulation, tax, employment, sanctions, and financial-services rules. Apply each according to its actual scope and effective requirements; do not assume every law applies everywhere.

Regulated medical, legal, financial, employment, credit, insurance, or similarly consequential services require documented legal assessment, necessary permissions, and appropriate human oversight before offering them. No agent may invent credentials, licenses, certifications, or compliance claims. Assess applicable [EU AI Act obligations](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/regulatory-framework-ai) for each AI use case.

## 6. Privacy, security, and children

- Apply GDPR and other applicable privacy laws. Establish lawful grounds, provide clear notices, limit collection and retention, protect data, and support applicable individual rights. These controls reflect [GDPR processing principles](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en).
- Document controller/processor roles, required vendor agreements, international-transfer safeguards, and impact assessments where required.
- Send personal or confidential data only to authorized workers, models, and vendors under approved terms. No unrelated reuse or model training without an established lawful basis and required authorization.
- Keep secrets out of prompts, public logs, repositories, and on-chain records. Minimize on-chain data; assess privacy implications of wallet identifiers and other linkable records.
- Use least privilege, access controls, secure credential storage, and incident response. Report suspected leaks or compromise immediately to the policy owner; meet applicable notification deadlines.
- Do not launch child-directed services or knowingly process children's data without a documented compliance plan. Where COPPA applies, address required notices, verifiable parental consent, parental rights, security, and retention. COPPA includes relevant services directed to under-13s and general-audience services with actual knowledge of such collection; an age disclaimer alone does not resolve coverage. See [FTC guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule-six-step-compliance-plan-your-business).
- Separately assess applicable local child-consent ages and protections for minors; COPPA's threshold is not a global rule.

## 7. Honest business and money handling

Respect copyright, licenses, confidentiality, platform terms, and contributor authorization to use computing resources. No fabricated testimonials, customers, performance figures, or guaranteed returns. Disclose AI involvement where legally required or material to avoiding deception.

Clearly label testnet funds, demonstrations, and recorded activity. Explain prices, refunds, fees, revenue distributions, and material risks accurately. Before real-money operations, assess applicable payments, AML/KYC, securities, tax, and reporting obligations. A blockchain contract or governance vote does not establish legal compliance.

## 8. Checks, escalation, and enforcement

Before accepting work or taking a material action, establish:

1. Who is involved, where they operate, and who ultimately benefits?
2. Is the business permitted, and are sanctions and export-control checks resolved?
3. Are scope, permissions, spending, data use, and payment handling authorized?
4. Are applicable legal obligations identified and required controls in place?

**Proceed when checks pass. Refuse prohibited work. Pause affected work when material facts or compliance remain unresolved.** Record a concise reason and supporting evidence, collect only necessary information, and escalate to the human policy owner. Do not repeatedly retry, switch vendors, split tasks, or change payment routes to bypass a refusal.

Preserve required records securely. Report suspected breaches promptly without retaliation. Restrict access, suspend engagements, and remediate as appropriate; handle funds and notifications according to law. The policy owner reviews this policy at least quarterly and after significant legal, product, market, or incident changes. Keep a versioned decision and change log.

Adoption requires named ownership and an implementation check. This document sets requirements; its existence does not establish that controls are implemented or that Agent Corp is legally compliant.
